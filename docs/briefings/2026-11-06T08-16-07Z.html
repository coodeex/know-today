👀 Keep an eye on

  • Managed project conversations can split parallel engineering work into threads and surface the points that require a human decision.
  • Cheap small models could make high-volume product features economical when quality and latency hold on the actual workload.
  • AI-generated dashboards become more usable for business decisions when every value exposes its inputs and formula.
  • Prompt-created developer extensions can turn recurring corrections into durable operating rules, provided teams measure whether they improve delivery.
  • Embodied conversational robots are reaching niche commercial pilots, but current pricing, availability, and biometric claims remain highly uncertain.

🧭 What changed

AI moves into structured work surfaces

Reported native document, spreadsheet, and presentation assistance can work with formatting, formulas, themes, and editable artifacts rather than only producing copyable text.

Coordination becomes a product capability

Project-level threading points toward an agent manager that decomposes work, tracks concurrent execution, and escalates only blocked decisions.

Cost changes the architecture

Lower-priced small-model claims make routing, observability, and workload-specific evaluation more important than choosing one universal model.

💭 Opinions worth testing

Opinion — Integrations that understand formulas, formatting, and themes are more valuable than generic connectors that only move text.
Opinion — Provenance is the trust boundary for AI analytics, because users need to inspect values and calculations before acting.
Opinion — Project-level agent coordination can remove a genuine bottleneck created by manually managing separate chats and tasks.
Opinion — Small-model economics can revive AI features that were previously too expensive to embed in a customer product.
Opinion — Persistent agent customizations become process debt unless they demonstrably improve a real workflow.
Opinion — Original ideas and a supplied design system remain necessary inputs for customer-facing AI output that does not feel generic.
Opinion — Output-first AI coding is valuable for rapid prototypes but is not a sustainable default for long-lived software.

Reflection: Demand structural awareness from integrations. Instrument model cost and quality. Keep only proven workflow extensions. Convert successful prototypes into owned engineering systems.

🔮 Predictions to track

Prediction — Software will become more personalized as users gain the ability to create their own interfaces and workflows with AI.
Prediction — Agent management will become increasingly multi-threaded, with better task decomposition, status visibility, and escalation.
Prediction — Lower cost per task will expand AI use into workflows that premium-model pricing could not justify.
Prediction — AI products will shift from systems people converse with toward systems people manage.
Prediction — Codebases built solely through output-first AI coding will become harder to evolve over time.

Reflection: Build routing into every AI product. Design for concurrent workstreams. Track escalation patterns. Measure maintenance cost after prototype promotion.

🚀 Build something ambitious

Autonomous operating cockpit for a vertical business

Outcome: Give a small company a continuously updated operating system that turns scattered work into decisions and completed actions.

Workflow: Ingest structured documents, spreadsheets, customer signals, and engineering work; trace key metrics to formulas and inputs; decompose opportunities into parallel agent workstreams; assign bounded authority for execution; escalate only policy, budget, or ambiguous-business decisions; feed outcomes back into reusable rules.

Capabilities: Workspace-aware agents, project orchestration, artifact dashboards with provenance, small-model routing, durable workflow instructions, and local or cloud execution environments.

First meaningful milestone: Run one weekly revenue-and-delivery cycle where the system produces a traceable operating brief, launches approved follow-up work, and reports completed outcomes.

Provenance-native decision intelligence

Outcome: Let teams ask operational questions without accepting opaque generated charts or hand-built reporting delays.

Workflow: Connect approved business data, generate editable dashboards, attach every metric to raw inputs and transformations, detect freshness gaps, and route anomalies to an agent that proposes and executes a scoped investigation.

Capabilities: Data connectors, formula-aware spreadsheet reasoning, dashboard artifacts, agent task threads, evaluation checks, and cost-aware model routing.

First meaningful milestone: Deliver a live weekly metrics board where every executive metric can be inspected to its input values and calculation logic.

⚡ Fast builds to try

  • Make a correction-to-rule collector: capture repeated agent feedback in a small rules file; get reusable instructions instead of repeating the same correction.
  • Make a token-cost status panel: add usage and estimated spend to your development workflow; get immediate visibility into which agent tasks deserve cheaper routing.
  • Make a formula-explainer sheet: ask an AI assistant to annotate a real spreadsheet’s inputs and formulas; get a quick test of whether its reasoning is inspectable.
  • Make a prototype promotion checklist: apply tests, ownership, architecture notes, and cleanup criteria to one AI-generated prototype; get a clear boundary between exploration and durable software.

⚠️ Caveats

  • Reported product names, availability, pricing, model comparisons, and integration behavior need independent verification before planning around them.
  • Visible formulas improve inspectability but do not guarantee fresh inputs, correct transformations, or sound business interpretation.
  • Claims around facial recognition and mood inference require rigorous accuracy, consent, biometric-data, and deployment review.
  • Prototype speed is not evidence of maintainability, reliability, or operational fit.

🧠 The author's perspective

The author believes that today’s incoming information aligns with the view that ambitious autonomy depends on a capable runtime, durable instructions, real integrations, decision data, and appropriately scoped authority: workspace-aware agents provide access to the structured surfaces where work actually happens, provenance-rich dashboards provide inspectable decision inputs, project threading provides a practical shape for planning and coordinating groups of agents, and cheaper small models make persistent high-volume execution more economically plausible. The reported ability to turn recurring corrections into durable rules also supports the idea that agent systems should learn operational practice over time, while the warning about unmanaged AI-generated code reinforces the need for harnesses that include implementation, testing, review, and deployment loops rather than isolated task completion.
